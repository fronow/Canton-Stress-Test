Where this comes from

A random pick fails when it lands on a holding you have already spent. The spent fraction of your pool grows from zero to f over a run, so the average failure rate is about f / 2, where f = transfers / holdings.

Measured against a CIP-0056 registry across an eightfold range of pool depth, predictions recorded before the runs: contention halved every time the pool doubled, with a mean error of about two percentage points. Raising concurrency fourfold moved it by less than one point — this is a bookkeeping effect, not a concurrency effect.

It under-predicts at high turnover, where genuine concurrent collisions start adding on top. Treat it as a good estimate and a floor, not an identity. Single participant, in-memory, one machine.

Estimates only, from a model measured on one registry. Your figures will differ — if they differ a lot, that is worth knowing and worth sharing.
